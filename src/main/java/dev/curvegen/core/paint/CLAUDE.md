# Paint

## Sources and block choices

- Resolve shape-specific fallbacks when reading paint settings so switching shapes keeps the player's choices. A missing fit defaults to Repeat Along on lines and Repeat elsewhere. Edge requires depth greater than one. On 3D equations, Wrap falls back to From Above and Along or Across gradients to Bottom to Top.
- Embed source snapshots under `pattern.`, `mix.`, and `gradient.` so presets survive library changes. Keep missing or malformed settings and incomplete sources at their current values. Clamp readable numbers and treat an unknown source as Off. Keep serialized pattern rows top first and grid coordinates bottom first. Preserve `-` for air or transparent image data, which can open, and `?` for an unusable captured block, which fills.
- Gradients preserve ideal step colours, block states, stops and manual swaps, including missing IDs. In saved steps, `!` marks a manual swap. Each step carries equal weight. Mixes save each cell with its weight after `*` and keep the shuffle seed in the settings. A mix derived from a pattern weights entries by frequency. The attachment vote ignores colour-only and empty entries, leaving the Blocks tab choice in charge when no blocks vote.
- `Ladder` caches by palette entry and family. A full block keeps its captured state when eligible. Shaped pieces try the material's variant before a colour match. `MaterialNames` supplies same-namespace variants for blocks missing from the game's family data. Fence choices stay in the Blocks tab fence's connection group. The default matcher compares face-average colours in CIELAB. The catalog also needs the source blocks' colours so ineligible blocks can match by colour. Status counts distinguish connector fallbacks from ordinary colour matches.
- One Material per Colour can change the full block to a material with the slabs and stairs the solve uses. Only those two shaped families determine whether a material qualifies. Other families keep their usual fallbacks.

## Coordinates and layouts

- Paint turns are anticlockwise in degrees, size is blocks per cell, and slide is in blocks. Repeat Along fits the height once across the strip unless the pattern turns or the strip closes across. On closed strips, Stretch keeps its seam at the start and repeating fits traverse whole repeats to remove the seam. For a loop closed only along its length, keep the requested scale across when the along correction is within a factor of two and feasible. For a closed cross-section, shear as needed and keep the loop windings nonzero and the determinant positive to avoid collapse or accidental mirroring.
- `Wrap2D` follows the centre line or the filled shape's outline for Edge, where the layer supplies `v`. Face paint projects filled shapes onto occupied bounds. Closed chains run anticlockwise from the bottom, or the back on floors, with the outside at low `v`. Open chains usually start at the left, and ordered Bézier input keeps its direction.
- Equation sampling can duplicate range edges, leave tiny gaps, or emit collapsed segments at exact zeros. Deduplicate either direction, drop segments whose endpoint keys coincide, and mend nearby open ends in input chain order. Joined chains retain existing ends, so the scan can move forward. Keep every resulting chain, since a tiny outline can still cover a whole block after stroke width is applied. Centre lines need only nearby candidates. Filled edges use sweeps followed by an exact search within 16 blocks, with buckets that cover segments beyond the grid. The sweeps suffice deeper inside, where many nearly equidistant perimeter segments would slow repainting.
- `Wrap3` reserves each level's non-air slot range to fill in parallel while preserving grid order. Wrap leaves block states oriented as captured. Projections use occupied bounds and turn full states anticlockwise viewed from `right × up`. Reverse `u` for right-handed shape axes to keep patterns readable in the menu and exports. Side projection follows the player's right, which swaps sides under a mirror. `Solve3Painter` retains the last mapping up to two million blocks, beyond which its float arrays would retain over 16 MB.
- Hash positions in shape-local coordinates excluding the 2D margin or 3D padding, so mixes and ragged gradients stay in place through turns and thickness changes. Patch sites and block centres are jittered to break straight boundaries. Ragged gradients nudge by up to a quarter band or one block, capped at one band so only adjacent steps mix. Along and Across give each 2D strip its own gradient span. A 2D gradient shares the face across layers.

## Capture and output

- Capture favours a one-block-thick depth axis, then the view. Use the eye's side of the box, or the look direction when inside its depth span. Choose up from the screen's up projected onto the capture plane, falling back to look when the projection is almost zero. The right axis is up × toward the viewer so the image reads without mirroring. Take the nearest non-air cell even when unusable, so a torch in front of a wall fills that cell.
- Image import reads ARGB rows top first. Weight pixels by coverage and alpha to suppress hidden colours, and average in linear light to avoid darkening fine black and white detail. Cells below half opacity are empty, and a wholly empty image makes no pattern. Quantize to 32 levels per channel to limit colour matching work.
- Use bundled translation keys for locale-independent file stems. The two-lane road is nine blocks wide for a flat Bézier line with Repeat Along.
- `Painted` uses `-1` for the Blocks tab choice and `OPEN` for holes. 2D slots retain the grid and can share one face across layers. 3D slots skip air. After opening holes `Solve3Painter.Done` compacts the slots against its settled result. Counts cover every layer and skip holes. Column zero is the Blocks tab choice. A 2D full block can have multiple entries when strips adjust their rotation independently. The 3D preview palette lists piece families by ordinal before painted entries.
